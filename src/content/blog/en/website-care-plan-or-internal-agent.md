Since DeeLabs does both, we get this question directly: *we could get our website properly sorted, or we could build an internal AI tool — which comes first?* It is a good question, and the honest answer is that for most Thai businesses the two options are not even competing. They sit at different heights of the same building. Here is how to tell which floor you should be working on.

## The one-question test

**Where does the work you want to improve actually happen?**

If the answer is *between you and your customers* — being found, being understood, being contacted, being booked — you have a front-door problem. That is the website care plan.

If the answer is *inside your operation* — hours lost every week to the same reading, drafting, checking, reconciling, and answering — you have a back-office problem. That is the internal agent.

| | Website care plan | Custom internal agent |
|---|---|---|
| Problem it solves | Customers can't find or reach you | Staff time burned on repetitive cognitive work |
| Who feels it daily | You, when the phone doesn't ring | Your team, every single week |
| Time to value | Days | 4–8 weeks (pilot) |
| Cost shape | Small, fixed, monthly (from 490 THB/month) | Scoped per pilot |
| Success looks like | More inquiries, more bookings | Hours saved, errors caught, faster responses |
| Risk | Low — you get a working site regardless | Bounded — a pilot either proves out or stops |

## When the website is unambiguously first

Choose the care plan first if any of these are true:

- Your current site is a template nobody has touched in a year, is slow on phones, or is down more than you'd like.
- Customers message you asking basic questions (hours, prices, location, bookings) that the website should have answered.
- You are paying for ads or LINE ads that land on a page that doesn't convert.
- Nobody owns the site — no updates, no backups, no one to call when it breaks.

The arithmetic is simple: an internal agent that saves your team five hours a week is worth little if the front door is so stale that new customers never arrive. Fix the funnel before optimising the kitchen.

## When the internal agent is unambiguously first

Choose the internal tool first if:

- Your website already works — it brings inquiries, and your team handles them.
- The bottleneck is *response capacity*: inquiries wait days, follow-ups slip, quotes take a week.
- A specific team (finance, service, ops) repeats the same document-heavy routine weekly and the volume is growing.
- You can name the job and a number — "AR follow-up takes 15 hours a week", "we answer 200 repetitive LINE questions daily".

Notice the pattern: the agent needs a *named job with a volume*. If you can't name it, you are not ready for the agent — and the website, or a LINE booking automation, is probably the real answer anyway.

## The sequencing that usually wins

For a typical growing Thai SME or brand:

1. **Site live on a care plan** — the front door works, looks right, and is maintained.
2. **Booking/inquiry automation on LINE** — inquiries get answered at 2 a.m. and payments get confirmed without staff.
3. **Then the first internal agent** — now you have real volume data showing exactly which back-office job deserves automating.

Each step funds the next, and each step produces the data that de-risks the following one.

## The exception nobody budgets for: distribution

One honest caveat cuts the other way. Some businesses with a genuinely excellent internal operation still starve, because their category is crowded and their front door is invisible — no search presence, no fresh content, no reason for anyone to land on their page. In that case the back office is not the bottleneck at all, and polishing it is a comfortable way to avoid the uncomfortable work of being findable. The test above still applies: if customers are not arriving, the answer is the front door — but the fix may be a care-plan site with real content, not another internal efficiency. Efficiency that nobody sees is a hobby; efficiency that meets arriving demand is a business model.

## The question to ask yourself tonight

Not "should we do AI?" — that question produces anxiety and decks. Ask instead: *when a customer wants us at 9 p.m. on a Sunday, what happens?* If the answer is "nothing happens until Monday", you need the website and the automation. If the answer is "customers arrive fine, but my team drowns on Tuesday", you are ready for a pilot on an internal tool.
