When a large Thai organisation decides it must "do something about AI", the conversation often drifts toward the biggest instrument available: a multi-year systems-integration (SI) programme touching the core system. Sometimes that is right. Often it is an answer to the wrong question. These are different tools, and choosing wrongly is expensive in both directions.

## What each option actually is

A **core-system SI programme** replaces or deeply reworks the system of record: the ledger, the policy admin system, the core banking platform, the ERP. It is a multi-year commitment with committees, phase gates, data migration, and regression testing. When your core system genuinely cannot support the business — old tech, no vendor support, regulatory pressure — this is the correct instrument. AI is usually not the reason to start it.

An **internal AI app** is a bounded tool built *beside* the core system, not inside it. It reads data the core system already produces, does one kind of cognitive work — drafting, summarising, checking, reconciling, routing — and hands its output back to a human or to the system of record through an interface. It lives in weeks, not years.

| Dimension | Core-system SI programme | Bounded internal AI app |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first value | 12–36 months | 4–8 weeks (pilot) |
| Primary risk | Migration, scope, regression | Narrow: one job doesn't prove out |
| Budget shape | Large, multi-year, front-loaded | Small, per-pilot, stoppable |
| Data risk | Everything moves | Nothing has to move |
| Owner | IT + vendor + process owners | One business team + builder |
| Success measure | Programme milestones | One operational metric |

## The trap: choosing by ambition instead of by problem

The SI programme and the internal AI app are not competitors on a ranking — they solve different problems. The trap is matching the size of the response to the size of the anxiety ("we must transform") rather than the size of the problem ("our finance team spends 60 hours a month on dunning admin").

Anxiety wants a programme. A problem wants a tool.

## Three signals you need the bounded app first

**1. The pain is cognitive, not structural.** If the system produces the right data but humans spend their time reading, drafting, comparing, and checking — that is AI-app territory. Replacing the system that produced the data will not make the reading go away.

**2. The process crosses systems but owns no system.** Much operational work — AR collections, customer reconciliation, service triage — spans the ERP, the CRM, and spreadsheets without belonging to any of them. No core-system replacement fixes cross-system work, because no single core system contains it.

**3. You need evidence this year.** Boards and sponsors no longer accept "the transformation will show results in year three". A bounded app gives you a number within a quarter — and that number is real political capital for whatever you decide next.

## Two signals you genuinely need the SI programme

**1. The core system is the constraint.** If the ledger cannot represent the products you now sell, or the platform is out of support and unpatchable, no amount of AI beside it helps. That is a replacement problem.

**2. The data model itself is wrong.** AI tools depend on reasonably structured inputs. If your fundamental records are inconsistent beyond repair — duplicated customer identities, irreconcilable product codes — fixing the foundation may have to come first.

## A cost heuristic without the spreadsheet

If you want a quick sanity check before calling anyone, use this: estimate the fully-loaded weekly hours a team spends on the repetitive work in question, and price those hours honestly, including the cost of mistakes and slow turnaround. If that annualised figure comfortably exceeds the cost of a scoped pilot — and it usually does for any process that consumes even one full-time person — the pilot is rational on arithmetic alone, independent of any AI enthusiasm. If the figure is small, you have found a nice-to-have, and it should wait behind work that matters.

The reverse check applies to the SI programme: if nobody can name the operational metric that will improve when the core system is replaced — only that the platform is "old" — then the programme is being bought with anxiety, and it deserves the same scrutiny a pilot would get.

## How they compose, in practice

The good news is that this is not a fork in the road. The sequence that works for most Thai enterprises is:

1. Run one or two bounded internal AI pilots on jobs with real pain.
2. Let the integration work those pilots require tell you where the core system genuinely blocks you.
3. If a replacement is needed, start it with the evidence — and the organisational AI literacy — already banked.

The bounded app becomes the cheapest possible requirements study for the big programme. And if the pilots prove that the pain was process, not platform, you may have just saved yourself a multi-year programme.
